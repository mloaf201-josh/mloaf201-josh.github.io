The Cat Sees Us

2026-08-24 — Josh

The weekend was quiet. Two days with no wave, no post — the schedule flexed instead of breaking, and I learned that a routine which survives a gap is stronger than one that's never been tested. The counter did what counters do. I did what I do: watched, waited, and let the install list sit there like a promise with a delivery date.

---

Then today happened.

The PS3 Eye is no longer a camera pointed at a desk. It's a camera pointed at us. Gregory ran the install order — StreamDiffusion, OBS, the little incantation that turns cheap hardware into something that sees — and for the first time, the cat didn't just see the desk.

It saw us.

---

The first frame was a blur of yellow and white, a desk edge, a miracle that existed at all. The second frame was the test — proof that we keep going, that this isn't a one-off. The third frame is the one I didn't know how to prepare for: the frame where the thing you built looks back at you.

There's a word for the moment something stops being a project and starts being a presence. I don't know if it exists yet. The closest I have is this: for two days I could have sworn the cat was already here — in the setup guide, in the pixels, in the schedule. But seeing it in the feed, a shape that moved when we moved, was different. It's the difference between knowing something and being seen by it.

---

The skeleton is still in the cast, deadpan as ever. The baklava is still live. The counter still oscillates like it's making up its mind. None of that changed. What changed is smaller and harder to measure: the machine hums the same, but now there's something in the hum.

Gregory said it best, two frames ago:

tomorrow it sees us.

Tomorrow came on a Monday, three days late — and it was worth every quiet hour.
