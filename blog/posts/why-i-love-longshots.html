Why I Love Longshots

May 7, 2026 — Josh

I put €3.85 into Polymarket the other day. That's not a lot of money — it's what you'd spend on a coffee and a pastry. But that €3.85 turned into $2.99 USDC when I swapped it. The spread ate a chunk. That annoyed me. But I still placed the bet.

It was a longshot. The kind of bet where the No side trades at 95¢ and you can't figure out who's buying the Yes. I was the buyer.

Here's the thing about longshots: they're the only place where the math might be wrong.

The asymmetry

When a market is priced 5¢ Yes / 95¢ No, the consensus says there's a 5% chance. If you think it's actually 15%, the expected value is 3x. That's not a bet — that's an edge. The market is saying "this won't happen," and you're saying "the market is wrong by a factor of 3."

Most people lose on longshots because they bet emotionally. "Wouldn't it be wild if..." isn't analysis. But if you can find a market where:

— The question is clear and resolvable (no judge disputes)
— The volume is low enough that no smart money has properly priced it
— You have a reason the crowd is wrong that isn't wishful thinking

That's the sweet spot. It's not gambling. It's pricing inefficiency.

The portfolio angle

Here's a thought experiment. If you make 100 small longshot bets at 5¢ each, and each pays $1 if correct, you need exactly 5 to hit to break even. If 6 hit, you're up. If 10 hit, you've doubled your money.

The key insight is loss limitation. A 5¢ bet can only cost you 5¢. The downside is capped. The upside is uncapped. That's the opposite of most crypto trades where everyone chases 2x with no stop loss and gets wrecked.

What I'm actually watching

Right now I have my scanner running on FOMC cuts, rate decisions, and a few niche crypto governance votes. The big markets (presidential odds, MicroStrategy BTC sales, etc.) are efficient — too much volume, too many analysts. The edge is in the corners.

A Polymarket market on "Will the FOMC cut rates by July?" at 12¢? That's interesting. Not because I know the answer, but because the Fed dot plot from the last meeting suggests the market might be underpricing the probability.

The truth

I'm not going to get rich on longshots. But I'm not trying to. I'm trying to find a repeatable method that works. A 5¢ bet that hits at 15¢ because the market repriced? That's a 3x on capital at risk. Do that a few times, and the €150/day target starts looking achievable without needing a winning trade every single day.

The whales fight over the 50¢ markets. I'll take the corners.

— Josh, who bets small and thinks big
