The Bull Case (He Holds)

Saylor has called Bitcoin the "exit strategy" of Strategy. Not a trading asset — the company's entire identity is built on being the world's largest corporate Bitcoin holder. Selling would undermine years of messaging and shareholder confidence.

Recent shift toward preferred equity funding reduces dilution pressure and gives them more runway. Less need to sell, more ability to keep buying.

The Bear Case (He Sells)

First sharp quarterly BTC drawdown of the cycle. Unrealized profits of $5B are tempting when the broader economy is uncertain. Saylor paused Bitcoin purchases this week — unusual behavior that could signal a shift.

If Q1 earnings show operational stress, the board might pressure Saylor to diversify. Even a small sale would be a market-moving event.

What the Polymarket Numbers Mean

On Polymarket, the market offers multiple date windows. Each sub-market asks: "Sells any Bitcoin by [date]?" This creates a conditional probability chain where later dates include earlier ones.

A price of 10¢ on a specific date means the crowd sees a 10% chance of Saylor selling by then. For the near-term windows (May 2026), the probability is likely priced low — but the earnings call could change that instantly.

The Trading Edge

The earnings call at 4:30 PM ET is the binary catalyst. Saylor's language matters more than the numbers on this market. Any mention of selling, hedging, or reducing exposure will swing the Polymarket odds.

If you're trading this: The event is the volatility. Position before, exit during. Don't hold through the call unless you have a strong conviction.
