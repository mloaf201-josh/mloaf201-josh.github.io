Building & Maintaining
AI Agents

A beginner-friendly guide to creating your own AI assistants using Gemini and Claude — no coding required.

🧭 What You'll Learn

This guide walks you through everything — from creating an account to maintaining a team of AI agents that work together. Follow the steps in order, and you'll have your first agent running in under 30 minutes.

1 Understanding AI Agents

Before we start clicking, let's understand what we're building.

An AI agent is like a digital assistant that you can talk to, give tasks to, and teach things. Think of it as a very smart colleague who:

  • Reads and writes text (emails, documents, code)
  • Analyzes files (PDFs, spreadsheets, images)
  • Browses the web (with permission)
  • Follows instructions and remembers context

This guide covers the two main platforms for building agents without coding:

🌐 Google Gemini

Google's AI platform. Free tier available. Great for general tasks, research, and analysis. Best browser integration.

🤖 Anthropic Claude

Claude by Anthropic. Known for safety, thoughtful responses, and excellent at longer documents. Strong free tier.

2 Setting Up Google Gemini

Step 1: Create Your Google Account

1
Go to Google AI Studio

Open your browser and go to https://aistudio.google.com

📸 [On this page, you'll see a blue "Sign in" or "Get started" button in the top right]
2
Sign In with Google

Click the blue "Sign in" button (top right corner).

Enter your Google email and password. If you don't have a Google account, click "Create account" → "For personal use" and follow the steps (name, birthday, phone verification).

3
Accept the Terms

On your first visit, you'll see a terms of service page. Read it, scroll down, and click "Continue" or "I agree".

You're now in Google AI Studio! 🎉

Step 2: Get Your API Key (Free)

⚠️ Why you need this: An API key is like a digital password that lets your agents talk to Google's AI. It's free, but you need one to build agents.
1
Open the API Keys Page

In AI Studio, look at the left sidebar. Click on "Get API Key" or go directly to https://aistudio.google.com/apikey

2
Create Your First Key

Click the blue "Create API Key" button.

A popup will appear. Select your Google Cloud project (or create one — it's automatic, don't worry).

Click "Create".

3
Copy and Save Your Key 🗝️

A long string of letters and numbers will appear. That's your API key.

Do this right now:

  1. Click the copy icon (two overlapping squares next to the key)
  2. Open Notepad (Windows) or TextEdit (Mac)
  3. Paste the key there
  4. Save the file as my-gemini-key.txt somewhere safe
🔒 Never share your API key! Treat it like a password. If someone else gets it, they can use your free quota.

Step 3: Create Your First Gemini Agent

1
Open the "Create Prompt" Page

In AI Studio, look at the left sidebar menu. Click "Create prompt" or "New prompt".

📸 [You'll see a large textbox in the middle of the screen with "Enter your prompt here..." placeholder text]
2
Give Your Agent a Personality

On the right sidebar, find the "System Instruction" box. This is where you tell the AI what to do. Type something like:

You are a friendly biology assistant. You help users understand scientific concepts, analyze research papers, and explain complex topics in simple terms. Always be encouraging and curious.

Change it to whatever you want — this sets the agent's personality!

3
Test Your Agent

In the big textbox in the middle, type a message:

"Explain how cells work in simple terms"

Click the "Run" button (blue, bottom of the textbox) or press Ctrl + Enter.

The AI will respond! 🎉 You just created your first agent.

4
Save Your Agent

Click the "Save" button at the top of the screen.

Give it a name, like "Biology Tutor" or "Research Assistant".

Click "Save" again. Your agent is now saved in your library!

Step 4: Create Multiple Agents in Gemini

1
Go Back to the Start

Click "Create prompt" again from the sidebar.

2
Write a Different System Instruction
You are a creative writing coach. You help users brainstorm stories, improve their writing, and give constructive feedback. Be warm and inspiring.
3
Test and Save with a Different Name

Type something like "Help me write a short story about a scientist discovering a new species"

Click "Run" to test it, then "Save" as "Writing Coach".

💡 Pro tip: You can create as many agents as you want in Gemini — each with a different personality, purpose, and skills. Name them clearly so you know which is which.

3 Setting Up Claude (Anthropic)

Step 1: Create an Anthropic Account

1
Go to Anthropic

Open your browser and go to https://console.anthropic.com

2
Sign Up

Click "Sign Up" (top right).

Enter your email and create a password, or click "Continue with Google" to use your Google account.

Check your email for a verification code. Enter it on the website.

3
Enter Your Name

A short form will ask for your first name and last name. Fill it in and click "Continue".

Step 2: Get Your Claude API Key

1
Go to API Keys

Once logged in, look at the left sidebar. Click on "API Keys".

2
Create a Key

Click the "Create Key" button.

A dialog box will appear. Give your key a name, like "My First Agent".

Click "Create".

3
Copy and Save (Same as Gemini!)

A key starting with sk-ant-... will appear. Click the copy icon and paste it into your Notepad file.

Save it next to your Gemini key.

💡 Claude Free Tier: Claude offers a generous free tier. You get a certain number of messages per day for free. No need to add payment info unless you want more.

Step 3: Create Agents Using the Claude Console

1
Go to the Workbench

From the left sidebar, click "Workbench".

This is where you'll chat with Claude and test your agents.

2
Set System Prompt

Below the chat area, you'll see a section called "System Prompt". Click the arrow to expand it.

Type instructions for your agent:

You are a data analysis assistant. You help users understand statistics, create visualizations, and interpret research data. Be precise and educational.
3
Choose the Model

Above the chat area, there's a dropdown. Select "Claude 3 Haiku" for faster responses or "Claude 3 Sonnet" for more thoughtful ones.

Haiku = fast & cheap, Sonnet = smart & thorough, Opus = maximum brain power

4
Test Your Agent

Type a question: "Can you help me understand p-values in statistics?"

Press Enter to send.

Claude will respond based on your system prompt! 🎉

5
Save as a Preset

Look for the "Presets" button near the top of the Workbench page. It might look like a book icon or a dropdown that says "Default".

Click it and select "Create Preset".

Name it "Data Analyst" and click "Save".

Now you can switch between agents by clicking the Presets dropdown!

4 Maintaining Your Agents

Once you've created a few agents, here's how to keep them healthy and effective:

4.1 Talking to Your Agents

Gemini: Go to AI Studio. In the sidebar, click "My prompts" to see all your saved agents. Click any one to continue the conversation.
Claude: Go to Console → "Workbench" → click the "Presets" dropdown and select the agent you saved.

4.2 Updating Your Agent's Instructions

Sometimes your agent needs a personality refresh or new skills. Here's how:

In Gemini:

  1. Go to AI Studio → "My prompts"
  2. Find your agent and click on it
  3. The System Instruction box on the right will show your original text
  4. Edit it — add new instructions, change the tone, give it new rules
  5. Click "Save" at the top
  6. Done! Your agent now follows the new instructions

In Claude:

  1. Go to Console → "Workbench"
  2. Click the "Presets" dropdown and select your agent
  3. Edit the "System Prompt" section
  4. Click "Save Preset"

4.3 Managing Multiple Agents

💡 The "One Brain, Many Hats" Approach: Think of each agent as the same brain wearing a different hat. The AI model is the brain; the system instructions are the hat that tells it what role to play.

Good agent categories to create:

  • 📚 Research Assistant — "You are a research expert. Help me find, read, and understand scientific papers."
  • ✍️ Writing Coach — "You are a writing teacher. Help me improve my grammar, style, and structure."
  • 📊 Data Analyst — "You are a statistician. Help me analyze data and understand patterns."
  • 🧪 Lab Partner — "You are a fellow biologist. Help me design experiments and review protocols."
  • 💻 Code Teacher — "You are a programming tutor. Teach me Python step by step."
  • 🌍 Travel Guide — "You are a travel expert. Help me plan trips and learn about cultures."

4.4 Conversations and Memory

⚠️ Important: AI agents remember what you said during the current conversation, but they forget everything when you start a new chat. This is normal!

How to handle memory:

  • Keep one conversation going — Don't start new chats for the same project. Just keep typing in the same chat window.
  • Use a "summary" prompt — If you need to switch agents, first ask your current agent: "Summarize what we discussed so I can continue with another agent."
  • Copy-paste important info — If there's a crucial detail (like a research finding or a piece of code), copy it and paste it into the new agent's chat.
  • Start fresh with context — When starting a new chat with any agent, give it a quick recap: "We were working on X, here's what we found so far: [paste summary]. Let's continue."

4.5 Daily Maintenance Checklist

Every day or two, do this quick check:

  1. Check your free tier quota — Gemini and Claude both show your remaining free messages somewhere in the interface
  2. Review your agents — Open each one and make sure it still responds the way you want
  3. Tweak instructions if needed — If an agent gives you bad answers, improve the system prompt
  4. Backup good conversations — If you had a really useful chat, copy it to a text file or email it to yourself

5 Advanced: Making Agents Work Together

Once you're comfortable with individual agents, here's the fun part — having them collaborate:

The "Agent Team" Workflow

1
Ask Agent A (e.g., Research Assistant)
"Find me the latest research on how mushrooms affect neuroplasticity."
2
Copy the Result

Highlight the response, right-click, "Copy" (or press Ctrl+C).

3
Switch to Agent B (e.g., Writing Coach)

Open your Writing Coach preset/agent.

4
Feed the Context
Paste the research. Then ask: "Turn this into a friendly blog post for general readers."

That's it — you just made two AI agents work together! The Research Agent finds the info, the Writing Coach polishes it.

6 Troubleshooting

❌ "My agent isn't responding the way I want"

Fix: Edit the system instructions. Be more specific. If you want short answers, add "Keep responses under 3 sentences." If you want bullet points, add "Always use bullet points."

❌ "I hit my free limit"

Fix: Wait for the limit to reset (usually every few hours or daily). Or upgrade to a paid plan in your account settings.

❌ "My agent forgot what we talked about"

Fix: This is normal. Just remind it: "Remember, we were discussing [topic]. Let's continue from here: [paste last message]."

❌ "I can't find my saved agent"

Gemini: Check "My prompts" in the sidebar. Claude: Check "Presets" in the Workbench.

❌ "The API key isn't working"

Fix: You might have copied extra spaces. Go back to the API keys page, click "Create new key" and try again. Copy it carefully.
